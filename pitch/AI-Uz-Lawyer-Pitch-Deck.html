PROBLEM

Legal information in Uzbekistan is hard to reach.

Uzbek law lives across dozens of codes, in two scripts and three languages, behind paywalls or PDFs. Citizens and small businesses pay for what should be a search query.

Fragmented corpus

27 separate codes — civil, criminal, tax, labour, family, land — each with hundreds of articles.

Language barrier

Statutes in Uzbek (Latin + Cyrillic) and Russian. Off-the-shelf NLP doesn't tokenize oʻ, gʻ, sh.

Lawyer cost gap

A 30-minute consultation costs more than the median weekly grocery budget for many.

Contract risk

SMEs sign templates pulled off random websites — no validation, no clauses, no checks.

RAG INTERNALS

Why hybrid search beats vectors alone.

Pure semantic search misses statutory keywords (article numbers, defined terms). Pure BM25 misses paraphrased intent. We run both and fuse the rankings.

Semantic

Voyage-4-large · cosine sim · ChromaDB
WINS AT
  • Paraphrased intent
  • Cross-language (RU↔UZ)
  • Conceptual matches
MISSES
  • Specific article numbers
  • Rare statutory terms

BM25 (custom)

Inverted index · Uzbek tokenizer
WINS AT
  • Exact statute citation
  • Domain vocabulary
  • Predictable scoring
MISSES
  • Paraphrasing
  • Conceptual queries
RRF FUSION

Best of both

score = 0.6 · (1/(60+rank_sem)) + 0.4 · (1/(60+rank_bm))

Each result is scored by how high it ranks in either pipeline. Rare-term hits and conceptual hits both bubble up — duplicates are merged by content hash + article ID.

Top 30 results then go to Voyage's rerank-2 model — final relevance is 0.7·rerank + 0.3·cosine.

ENGINEERING

Off-the-shelf tokenizers don't speak Uzbek.

Uzbek Latin uses oʻ, gʻ, ʼ — characters that standard whitespace tokenizers either drop or split. We wrote a tokenizer that treats them as letters.

DEFAULT TOKENIZER

loses Uzbek characters

>>> tokenize("Oʻzbekiston Konstitutsiyasi")
["O", "zbekiston", "Konstitutsiyasi"]

>>> tokenize("gʻalla yetishtirish")
["g", "alla", "yetishtirish"]
OUR UZBEK TOKENIZER

preserves diacritics

_UZBEK_TOKEN = re.compile(
  r"[a-zA-ZoʻgʻOʻGʻ'ʼ]+", re.UNICODE)

>>> tokenize("Oʻzbekiston Konstitutsiyasi")
["oʻzbekiston", "konstitutsiyasi"]

Result: BM25 actually finds statutes when the user types them in their natural script.
